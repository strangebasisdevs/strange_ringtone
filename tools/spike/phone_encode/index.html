Phone encode spike

Can this phone encode a ringtone by itself? This page synthesises a short loop, encodes it to AAC with the browser's own encoder, wraps it in an M4A file written in JavaScript, and offers it to the share sheet. No audio leaves the phone and no server takes part.

A real song

The loop is too simple to show what bit rate the encoder really gives. Pick an audio file and the page encodes 30 seconds of it four ways: at a constant and at a variable rate, at a high and at a low bit rate. The song stays on the phone, and the report doesn't name it.

    What to check: the test tones

    1. Tap Make the test tones. Each tone gets a player: does it play to its end? (Each starts with a tenth of a second of silence, on purpose.)
    2. For P1, tap Share. Does the share sheet offer Use as Ringtone, and does iOS accept the tone?
    3. In Settings, Sounds & Haptics, Ringtone: is P1 listed, and does it play to its end?
    4. Do the same for P3, which is exactly 30.0 s, the longest iOS accepts.
    5. Try P2 only if P1 was refused: it is the same audio without an edit list.
    6. If the share sheet doesn't offer it, tap Download and share the file from Safari's downloads list.
    7. Tap Copy report and send the report back, with what you saw.

    Afterwards, remove the test tones by swiping left on them in the Ringtone list.

    What to check: the song

    1. Pick an audio file with a busy stretch of music, set where the 30 seconds start, and tap Encode the song.
    2. With headphones, play S1 and S2, the two at the high rate. Does either sound worse than the song itself?
    3. Play S3 and S4, the two at the low rate. Can you hear a difference from S1 and S2?
    4. Share the one you like best and set it as a ringtone: does iOS accept it?
    5. Tap Copy report and send the report back, with what you heard.
